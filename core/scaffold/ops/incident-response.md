# Incident response ({{PROJECT_NAME}})

Generated by `eos scaffold ops`; yours to edit. Short on purpose: you read this while something is on fire.
Adapted from the PagerDuty Incident Response docs (Apache-2.0, https://response.pagerduty.com/) and the
Google SRE books (read, not copied: https://sre.google/sre-book/managing-incidents/).

## When is it an incident?
Declare one when any of these is true: users cannot pay, sign in or reach the product; data may be lost,
wrong or exposed; money totals do not match; a fast-burn SLO alert is not mitigated within 15 minutes.
If unsure, declare it. Closing a false alarm is cheap; a late declaration is not.

## Severity
| Sev | Meaning | Examples | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| SEV-1 | Critical: most users affected, money or data at risk, security breach | API down; double charges; leaked data | Page now, all hands, status page update within 30 min |
| SEV-2 | Major: many users or a key journey degraded | Mobile-money callbacks failing; sign-in errors for one region | Page now, status page if users notice |
| SEV-3 | Minor: few users, workaround exists | One report export slow | Ticket, fix in working hours |
The response times are starting defaults to tune, not an industry standard. Anything SEV-2 or worse is a
major incident. If unsure between two levels, pick the higher one; you can
downgrade later.

## Roles (one person may hold several in a small team; never the commander and the fixer for long)
- Incident commander: decides, delegates, keeps the timeline honest. Does not debug.
- Fixer (subject-matter expert): investigates and mitigates.
- Communicator: status page, support, stakeholders, on a fixed schedule.
- Scribe: writes the timeline (UTC) as it happens.
Solo developer: you are all four. Write the timeline anyway (one line per action) and tell a named
backup person that you are on it.

## First 15 minutes
1. Declare: open the incident channel/issue `INC-YYYYMMDD-short-name`, set severity, name the commander.
2. Stop the bleeding before finding the cause: roll back the last deploy, turn off the last flag,
   fail over, or shed load. Follow the alert's runbook (docs/runbooks/).
3. Protect evidence: do not delete logs or rotate away the data you need; never copy production
   personal data to laptops or chat.
4. Communicate (template below), then every 30 minutes for SEV-1, 60 for SEV-2, even if "no change".

## Status update template
> [Investigating | Identified | Monitoring | Resolved] {{PROJECT_NAME}}: <what users see>, since <time UTC>.
> Affected: <who / which regions / which payment methods>. Workaround: <if any>.
> Next update by <time UTC>.
Say what users see, not internal guesses. No blame, no names, no unconfirmed causes.

## Closing
Resolved = the user impact is gone and monitoring confirms it, not "the fix is merged". Then: postmortem
draft within 5 working days for SEV-1/SEV-2 (docs/postmortems/_template.md), action items with owners,
and an update to the runbook that was missing or wrong.

## Security and personal-data incidents
Also follow docs/security/ (if scaffolded) and your legal duties: data-protection laws may require
notifying a regulator or the people affected within a deadline. Decide that with a human who knows
the law in your jurisdictions; do not guess.
