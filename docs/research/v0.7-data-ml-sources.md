## 2. Claims deliberately not made
- **No prices** for warehouses, BI seats, managed orchestrators or analytics vendors. Cost advice is structural: bytes scanned, auto-suspend, quotas, partition pruning, dry runs. It names the vendor pages without amounts.
- **No benchmark speed claims** ("DuckDB is N× faster than …"). Nothing citable and current was verified, so the refs describe fit (in-process, single writer) instead.
- **No legal conclusions.** The Cameroon, EU, GDPR/CJEU and US texts are quoted and dated. The refs say what to check and who should check it, not whether a given product complies. The Cameroon text comes from OCR and must be checked against the official PDF.
- **No claim that pseudonymised or hashed data is anonymous**, and no k value that is "safe". The refs give WP216's three tests instead.
- **No specific drift thresholds as recommendations.** Evidently's defaults are reported as defaults. Teams set alert thresholds from their own history.
- **No fairness metric picked for the user.** The refs explain the trade-off and the incompatibility results. Choosing a metric is a product, legal and ethics decision.
- Removed during review: the claim that "DVC is slow with many small files" (not verified), a model-card year, and "Linux Foundation project" for Bitol (it is LF AI & Data). lakeFS was first written as Apache-2.0 and corrected to BSL 1.1 after reading the LICENSE.
- The EDPB consultation page (obsolete), an old CNIL URL (404) and a Segment HTML page (403) were dropped or replaced by the live pages above.

## 3. Enforcement decisions
- **`eos data check`** runs in-process with no tools. Contracts are `*.odcs.yaml|yml|json` anywhere, plus YAML/JSON under `contracts/` that declares `kind: DataContract`, or the globs in `data.contracts`. YAML is parsed with PyYAML when installed, otherwise with a small built-in subset parser that refuses anchors, tags, tabs and multi-document files with a clear message. On the scaffold's contract it gives the same result as PyYAML (tested). The rules:
  - DAT001: not valid YAML/JSON, or not a mapping.
  - DAT002: `apiVersion`, `kind`, `id` or `version` missing, `kind` not `DataContract`, or `apiVersion` not like v3.2.0.
  - DAT003: `version` is not a quoted semantic version.
  - DAT004: schema objects or properties without a name, an invalid `logicalType`, or a property listed twice. A missing `logicalType` is a warning.
  - DAT005 (warning): a property named like personal data (email, phone, MSISDN, national ID, date of birth, names, address, precise location) without `classification`.
  - DAT006: with `--base`, a breaking change without a major version bump. Breaking means a removed object or property, a changed `logicalType`, optional → required, or narrower `enum` values. A non-breaking schema change that keeps the same version is a warning.
  - DAT007: two contracts share an id.
  - DAT008 (warning): no `team`.
  - Notebooks are the git-tracked `*.ipynb` files. NB000: invalid JSON. NB001: outputs present. NB002: execution counts present. NB003: a secret-like value (the LLM secret patterns plus AWS access keys and Slack tokens). NB004 (warning): larger than `data.notebook_max_kb`, default 500, which matches pre-commit's `check-added-large-files --maxkb=500`.

  Exceptions go in `.eos/config.json` → `data.allow`, each with a written reason.
- **`eos data split`**: stdlib CSV checks across train/validation/test files:
  - SPL001: an entity id appears in more than one split.
  - SPL002: identical rows appear in more than one split.
  - SPL003: the splits are out of time order, or the gap is shorter than `--gap-days`.
  - SPL004 (warning): a single column determines the label for at least 99% of training rows (only checked with 50+ rows, and only for columns whose distinct values are at most half the rows).
  - SPL005 (warning): outcome-like column names (chargeback, refund date, write-off, days past due…).

  It also prints row counts, target rate and date range per split. It cannot see leakage inside feature computation; the checklist covers that.
- **Lanes**:
  - trivial/spike: nothing runs;
  - standard: checks changed contracts and notebooks (including DAT006 against the lane base), plus a note to run `dbt build --select state:modified+` when dbt files change;
  - risky/one-way-door/`--full`: checks every contract and notebook;
  - `eos check` runs `data check` when contracts or tracked notebooks exist.

  `*.odcs.*` files are now public contracts in lane classification. Removing lines from one makes the change one-way-door, the same as OpenAPI/proto files.
- **`eos scaffold analytics`** generates:
  - a dbt project on DuckDB with synthetic XAF seeds, staging views, and two marts;
  - 17 data tests, 1 unit test, an enforced contract on `fct_daily_payments`, and a singular reconciliation test (money in the mart equals money in staging);
  - telemetry turned off (`send_anonymous_usage_stats: false`), and a local and CI target that need no secrets;
  - an ODCS v3.2.0 contract for the mart;
  - a CI workflow (SHA-pinned checkout and setup-uv; dbt-core and dbt-duckdb pinned from `versions.toml`; `eos data check --base`);
  - 4 Semgrep rules for personal data sent to analytics SDKs (property names and values; Python and JS/TS; PostHog, Segment, Mixpanel, Amplitude, RudderStack, Heap, gtag, Firebase `logEvent`).

  dbt v1 is pinned because the v2 engine does not list Postgres. DuckDB keeps CI offline.
- **`eos scaffold ml`**: `docs/ml/split-leakage-checklist.md` (framing, split, point-in-time features, sanity checks, notebook hook), plus model-card and datasheet templates.
- **Not enforced, on purpose**:
  - Choosing a warehouse or orchestrator is a scale and team decision.
  - Fairness metrics and thresholds are policy decisions.
  - Drift thresholds come from the team's own data.
  - Anonymisation cannot be proven by a linter; the refs teach the tests and point to DP libraries.
  - `datacontract-cli`, Great Expectations and Soda stay optional (Soda's licence is ELv2).
  - nbstripout is suggested, not installed. `eos data check` catches outputs whether or not the hook is installed.

## 4. Human review needed
- Legal and compliance: the Cameroon Law 2024/017 quotes (OCR), the EU AI Act and Digital Omnibus dates, SCHUFA, Regulation B, and the CNIL conditions in `ml/fintech-risk` and `analytics/product-events`. A lawyer should confirm them before any credit-scoring or consent decision.
- Money code: the scaffold's reconciliation test and the mart's money columns are examples. Whoever owns finance data must confirm the definitions (refund dating, failed attempts) before dashboards use them.
- Thresholds: SPL004's 99% purity and 50-row minimum, NB004's 500 KB, and the DAT005 name list are starting points that a data lead should confirm.
- Security: the PII-in-events Semgrep rules are pattern-based and miss renamed variables and custom wrappers. A security reviewer should read them before teams rely on them.
